## Design Context

### Users

Django developers building and debugging server-driven HyperDjango applications. They use the website to learn through working examples and use development tooling to quickly connect a browser interaction to Python execution, streamed results, and concrete DOM changes.

### Brand Personality

Technical, direct, and confident. The product should feel like a precise developer instrument: dense where useful, fast to scan, and free of ornamental chrome.

### Aesthetic Direction

Industrial editorial minimalism shared with the HyperDjango website: hard black dividers, light neutral surfaces, IBM Plex Mono and Doto typography, restrained blue/green/red/yellow semantic accents, square geometry, and no shadows. Debug tooling owns its assets and Shadow DOM so host-page styles cannot alter it. Text in data views remains unwrapped and uses contained horizontal scrolling when necessary.

### Design Principles

1. Make causal relationships and actual outcomes more prominent than decorative summaries.
2. Keep every panel structurally contained; wide diagnostic data scrolls inside its owning section and never breaks the drawer grid.
3. Preserve high information density with a consistent 4px spacing scale and strong dividers.
4. Adapt the debugger to its context: a compact movable launcher, a half-screen bottom drawer by default, and an explicit fullscreen mode.
5. Prefer observable facts, clear states, keyboard access, and reduced-motion support over visual effects.
